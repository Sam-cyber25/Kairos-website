## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Local businesses in Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh — shops, institutes, clinics, restaurants, service providers — that currently have no web presence or an embarrassing one. Primarily small/micro businesses, owner-operated.

**Decision-makers:** The business owner themselves. No procurement process. Usually one person who Googled their problem or got a WhatsApp referral.

**Primary use case:** Getting a professional website that makes a local business look credible and findable on Google — so customers call instead of scroll past.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "Help me look like a real, trustworthy business online"
- "Help my customers find me when they search Google"
- "Build something I'm not embarrassed to show people"

**Use cases:**
- Brand-new website for a business with zero web presence
- Replacement for a dated/broken WordPress site
- Landing page + contact info for a service business
- Portfolio / credibility page for a local professional (tutor, trainer, consultant)

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Local Kanpur businesses are invisible online. Potential customers search on Google, find nothing (or find a competitor), and call someone else. The business doesn't even know what it's losing.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Cheap freelancers deliver WordPress templates that look like everyone else's — no differentiation
- Big agencies are too expensive and too slow; they don't understand the local market
- DIY tools (Wix, etc.) produce results the owner can't maintain and is quietly embarrassed by
- Word of mouth alone isn't scaling; digital is now table stakes

**What it costs them:** Lost customers every week who searched online and found a competitor instead. Businesses operating below their potential because they look smaller than they are.

**Emotional tension:** Embarrassment ("I know my website looks bad and I don't know how to fix it"), anxiety ("what if I spend money and it doesn't work"), and quiet resignation ("maybe my kind of business just doesn't need one").

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Local Kanpur freelancers and small agencies — fall short because they produce cheap templates, take too long, don't communicate, and hand over something the owner can't update or trust.

**Secondary:** DIY platforms (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify) — fall short because the result looks amateur, the owner gets stuck, and there's no real design thinking applied.

**Indirect:** Doing nothing / relying on JustDial, Google Maps profile only — falls short because it provides no credibility, no differentiation, and no owned digital presence.

**Kairos's edge:** Custom code, Sam's taste and craft, 3–5 day turnaround, WhatsApp-first communication (how Kanpur businesses actually work), and a founder who cares about the work as a matter of personal identity — not just a transaction.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push (frustration with current situation):**
- Embarrassed by their current site (or absence of one)
- A competitor just launched a site and is getting more calls
- Someone told them they couldn't find the business online
- Existing site is broken or looks 10 years old

**Pull (what attracts them to Kairos):**
- Fast turnaround — real results in a week, not months
- WhatsApp communication — no intimidating agency process
- Price is visible and fair, not hidden behind a "get a quote" wall
- Someone who actually understands Kanpur

**Habit (what keeps them stuck):**
- "It's worked fine without a website so far"
- Assumed a website would be too expensive or complicated
- Not sure what to provide or how the process works

**Anxiety (what worries them about switching):**
- Will I actually get what I paid for?
- Will I need to keep paying forever?
- What if I can't maintain it after it's built?
- What if something breaks?
